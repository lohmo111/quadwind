PPO: learning in small, careful steps

Actor

observation → action (policy π)

Critic

observation → “how much reward is ahead” V

Advantage A

better than the critic expected? → do it more often

Loop: collect experience from 16 drones → compute A (GAE) → several epochs of updates → repeat

A > 0, good action: reinforce it, but by no more than ×1.2

A < 0, bad action: weaken it, but to no less than ×0.8

x-axis: r = π(new) / π(old); y-axis: the L-CLIP objective. No gradient in the shaded zone — the policy can’t “run away”.

Plots after Fig. 1 of Schulman et al., 2017, “Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms”

[Speaker B] Thanks! Building block one — PPO. It has two networks. The actor is the pilot: it looks and picks an action. The critic is the coach: it estimates how much reward lies ahead. If things went better than the critic expected, we do that more often; if worse — less often. PPO’s key trick is on the plots: the policy isn’t allowed to change too sharply. The ratio of the new policy to the old one is clipped to a corridor from 0.8 to 1.2. That keeps training stable, without jumps.
